# Geometry benchmark ledger

## Purpose and evidence boundary

This ledger records the M5.7 independent verification of AeroForge schema-v3 parametric geometry, closed render lofts, and model-v1 computational lifting-surface panels. Expected values are constants or equations evaluated in the validation fixture; they are not generated by the production functions under test. The suite verifies geometry only. It does not establish aerodynamic convergence, VLM accuracy, flightworthiness, or certification suitability.

Reproducible inputs are in `../src/validation/fixtures/m5_7GeometryBenchmarks.ts`; independent assertions are in `../src/validation/m5_7GeometryVerification.test.ts`. Production equations remain in `../src/engineering/wingGeometry.ts`, `fuselageGeometry.ts`, `tailGeometry.ts`, `controlSurfaces.ts`, and `analysisGeometry.ts`.

## Coordinate and reference conventions

- SI units throughout. Aircraft `x` is aft, `y` is starboard, and `z` is up.
- Wing and horizontal-tail source stations run root-to-starboard tip; symmetric geometry is mirrored about `y=0`. Vertical-tail stations run upward.
- Station span coordinates are projected aircraft `y` distances for horizontal surfaces and aircraft `z` distances for the vertical tail. Dihedral changes station `z`; it does not rescale the authoritative projected span.
- Positive wing twist raises the leading edge about the station quarter chord. The quarter-chord point is invariant under twist.
- Projected reference area integrates chord over projected span. Computational panel area is the triangulated three-dimensional mean-camber surface area. These are intentionally different quantities.
- Horizontal panel normals point generally `+z`; conventional vertical-tail normals point generally `+y`. Bound-vortex endpoints always follow increasing physical span coordinate: port-to-starboard for horizontal surfaces and base-to-tip for the vertical tail.

## Analytical reference equations

For a linear-chord section of projected span `d`, root chord `c0`, and tip chord `c1`:

```text
S = d (c0 + c1) / 2
I_c2 = d (c0² + c0 c1 + c1²) / 3
MAC = sum(I_c2) / sum(S)
AR = b² / S
```

For a linearly varying quarter-chord coordinate `q0` to `q1`, its area-weighted reference is:

```text
q_bar = d (2 c0 q0 + c0 q1 + c1 q0 + 2 c1 q1) / (6 S)
```

Fuselage references are `A_circle=πd²/4`, `A_ellipse=πwh/4`, `V_cylinder=πr²L`, and `V_ellipsoid=4πabc/3`. The cylinder wetted benchmark includes both end caps: `2πrL+2πr²`.

## Computational panel audit

The flat rectangular case uses one chordwise panel and one spanwise panel per physical half. Independent corner arithmetic gives `10 m²` per panel, centroid `(1, ±2.5, 0) m`, quarter-panel-chord vortex `x=0.5 m`, three-quarter-panel-chord collocation `x=1.5 m`, chord direction `+x`, span direction `+y`, and normal `+z`. AeroForge reproduces every value exactly.

The audit additionally verifies:

- semantic corner order and two-triangle area independently of the production area function;
- unit normal magnitude and declared `+z`/`+y` orientation;
- port and starboard bound vortices both oriented from lower to higher global span coordinate, avoiding an accidental mirrored circulation sign;
- port/starboard shared-root edge equality, unique panel signatures, no edge with more than two owners, and a connected surface;
- exact station, control-span, and hinge boundaries; exactly one trailing-edge panel per span interval;
- stable surface/span/chord panel ordering and byte-for-byte deterministic repeated generation;
- finite cambered/twisted geometry, positive orientation, invalid-setting rejection, and neutral-only control warnings;
- vertical-tail bound vortices oriented base-to-tip;
- render-loft station coordinates matching computational station boundaries, finite render normals, and watertight closed render topology.

### Control sign convention

A horizontal control instance's hinge axis is root-to-tip. It therefore points `+y` on starboard and `-y` on port. Differential ailerons use the same local right-hand rotation angle on both sides and consequently move their trailing edges in opposite global `z` directions. Linked flaps/elevators reverse the port local angle so both sides move in the same global direction. A future solver must consume the resulting physical geometry or apply this local-axis convention; it must not infer global deflection sign from the scalar command alone.

## Geometric refinement studies

### Mean-camber lifting surface

Representative symmetric wing: half-span `5 m`, chords `2.4→1.1 m`, leading edge `1→2.3 m`, dihedral `7°`, twist `+4→-5°`, airfoils NACA 2412→4412. The analytical projected area remains exactly `17.5 m²` at every level.

The fine three-dimensional mean-surface area is `1.149%` above projected reference area because of camber, twist, sweep, and dihedral. This difference is geometric, not discretization error, and is never substituted into the aerodynamic reference metrics.

### Fuselage

For the closed `4 m × 2 m` cylinder, 12/24/96 radial segments give wetted areas `30.8466283298`, `31.2726859887`, and `31.4069560132 m²`, monotonically approaching `10π=31.4159265359 m²`. Volume and centroid are quadrature results independent of render radial refinement and remain invariant.

For the station-defined spheroid, 16/32/128 axial station intervals give volumes `12.4284106199`, `12.5284323481`, and `12.5635817694 m³`, monotonically approaching `4π=12.5663706144 m³`. This measures convergence of the station representation plus its documented smooth interpolation; it is not a claim that a small arbitrary station set exactly represents an ellipsoid.

## Integration and regression audit

- Schema-v1 trapezoid and scalar fuselage/tails migrate to schema v3 with analytical wing area preserved; missing legacy controls become an empty model-v1 collection.
- Schema-v2 wing geometry migrates without changing wing reference properties; scalar fuselage/tails migrate to component-v1 stations.
- The full M4 sizing/candidate suite remains in the release run. Candidate previews deep-copy fuselage and tail station arrays, preserve normalized control coverage, never mutate the authoritative design, and detect source/sizing/input changes.
- Store regression proves an authoritative geometry edit increments `design.revision`; retained computational meshes then report stale. Mesh generation itself does not mutate the design.
- Existing analytical aerodynamics, stability, structures, performance, and preliminary sizing continue to consume projected references and retain their previously documented low-fidelity limitations. No new coefficient, derivative, mass, or force was inferred from complex geometry.

## Corrected defect

M5.7 found one validation-order defect. When a selected parent wing was invalid and a control surface existed, analysis-mesh generation recorded the parent error and then entered control validation, which sampled the invalid wing and threw. The correction stops dependent control validation after selected-parent failure and converts any remaining parent-sampling exception into a structured `invalid-parent-geometry` result. No production geometry equation or valid-case numerical result changed.

## VLM geometry readiness assessment

**Decision: ready for M7 VLM formulation within the declared conventional, neutral-control scope; ready for M6 airfoil/polar work.** The contracts provide deterministic corners, local frames, panel normals, collocation points, quarter-chord finite bound segments, trailing-edge flags, station/control identifiers, and consistent global bound-vortex orientation. They can support either a horseshoe lattice or a vortex-ring lattice provided the solver explicitly defines circulation sign, wake direction, panel-neighbor construction, and force/moment frames.

The following are not blockers for a neutral conventional-wing first solver, but must remain explicit:

- no wake nodes, wake legs, panel-neighbor indices, strip identifiers, or closed vortex-ring connectivity are stored; these belong to the future solver/topology layer;
- bound vortices are panel-local quarter chord and collocation points are panel-local three-quarter chord, as required by the current thin-lattice convention;
- port vortices are globally port-to-starboard, not root-to-tip; a solver must not reverse them when iterating each half independently;
- only neutral control geometry is supported; deflected lattices, control gaps, and aerodynamic control effectiveness remain unavailable;
- this M5.7 benchmark used mean camber derived only from supported NACA four-digit sections; M6.1 subsequently added imported coordinate and polar provenance without changing these benchmark cases;
- vertical-tail `+y` orientation is conventional for the current single fin and must be handled consistently in force/moment transforms;
- unconventional/canted/twin/V tails, curved rigid hinges across station breaks, complex high-lift devices, fuselage interference, and body lattices remain outside scope;
- render surfaces are closed thickness lofts while computational surfaces are thin mean-camber lattices by design; their areas are not interchangeable.

M5 is complete for the approved conventional aircraft geometry foundation. Geometry export, persisted history, unconventional configurations, and deflected analysis lattices remain later backlog items and do not invalidate this bounded completion decision.
